# Sus Party — Content Plan

TikTok/Reels chaos style. Semi-organized filming at the mansion. Goal: enough raw material for 4–8 weeks of posting without manufacturing fake moments.

---

## Core insight

Your game generates its own content. Social deduction produces betrayal, accusation, and panic on camera for free — you don't need skits. The mansion session's job is to **capture reactions, not gameplay**. Screens are boring; faces are not. Every idea below is built on that.

The one mechanic to lead with: **the Sonos house-wide meltdown alarm.** Nothing else in the party-game space has "the whole mansion starts screaming at you." That's your signature shot.

---

## Video ideas (ranked by viral odds)

### Tier 1 — post these first

**1. "We turned a mansion into Among Us" (anchor video)**
The one-sentence premise carries itself. 30–45s: room code on phones → roles revealed → sprinting through halls → a kill → meeting bell through Sonos → someone gets ejected screaming. End on the URL. This is the pinned video and the one you re-edit in 3 variants (different kills, different endings).

**2. The meltdown alarm hits**
Static shot of a calm room — people doing tasks, chatting. Alarm erupts from the Sonos. Everyone's head snaps up, then chaos as they sprint. No context needed, no voiceover. Caption: "the reactor is melting down and it's in the kitchen speaker too." Sound-driven clips travel well; the alarm audio itself could become a reusable sound.

**3. Kill POV**
Intruder's phone in frame: they walk up to a friend mid-task, both phones out, kill confirmed, victim's face drops as their screen goes red/dead. The victim's reaction IS the video. Get 5+ of these — every kill is a unique clip.

**4. The betrayal reveal**
Someone vehemently defends themselves in a meeting → gets voted out → ejection screen shows they were the intruder (or worse, innocent). Film every meeting from a corner; you'll get 2–3 of these per night. Caption format: "she swore on her life" / "we ejected the wrong guy."

**5. The taunt notification**
Player quietly doing a task, phone buzzes, intruder taunt appears on screen, they look around the empty room genuinely unsettled. Horror-adjacent, very shareable. ("the killer can send you messages and I want to go home")

### Tier 2 — steady posting material

**6. "POV: you're the intruder"** — first-person-ish walk through the house, passing oblivious friends, fake-doing a task, picking a target. Tension format, works as a series.

**7. Dead player purgatory** — the dead can't talk. Shot of ghosts sitting on a couch watching the living accuse the wrong person, physically vibrating, unable to say anything. Extremely relatable comedy.

**8. Meeting cam supercut** — 3–4 unhinged accusation clips back to back. "evidence" in these games is always insane ("he was BREATHING weird"). Endless series potential.

**9. Task list comedy** — real tasks are user-written and absurd. Screen-record scrolling the list, or film someone earnestly completing "eat beans" while a murderer roams the house. Also doubles as a soft tutorial for the customization feature.

**10. Kill selfie / death card reveal** — if the selfie-on-player-card flow produces funny frozen faces, a "cause of death photo" compilation writes itself.

**11. "Party games where you SIT are over"** — light manifesto/voiceover over the best chaos b-roll. Positions the game vs. Jackbox/couch games. Good for week 3–4 when you have footage depth.

**12. Rules in 30 seconds** — one clean explainer to link in comments of everything else. Not viral bait; conversion infrastructure. Make it once, make it good.

### Formats to skip

Talking-head dev logs (wrong audience for this game), staged skits with acting (this is the slop you're worried about), gameplay screen recordings with no faces, anything over 60 seconds.

## Mansion footage list

Realistic for "both, but not super organized": rig fixed phones once at the start, give players one instruction, and have one floater. That's it.

### Setup before the first round (15 min, do once)

- [ ] **3–4 fixed phones/GoPros on tripods or leaned on shelves**, landscape or portrait-consistent, in: the meeting room, the busiest hallway, the kitchen, and one task-heavy room. Hit record, leave them for the whole session, harvest later. This is where the betrayal reveals and alarm reactions come from with zero effort.
- [ ] **One shot of the Reactor screen location** — TV with the game up, room code visible (use a burner code or blur later).
- [ ] **Exterior shot of the mansion**, day and night. 10 seconds each. Establishes "we rented a mansion for this" — that context alone boosts every video.
- [ ] Tell every player: **"if something insane happens to you, pull out your camera after and give a 10-second reaction."** That's the whole instruction. Selfie-mode confessionals right after a kill/ejection are gold and require no coordination.

### Must-get shots (floater's checklist, roughly priority order)

1. **Meeting bell / meltdown alarm going off + the room's reaction** — every single time it happens. Highest-value 5 seconds of the night.
2. **Every meeting, filmed wide from a corner** — full discussion and vote. You only need 2–3 good ones but you can't predict which.
3. **At least 3 kills from the killer's side** (follow an intruder for a round) and **2 from the victim's side**.
4. **Ejection reactions** — the moment the vote lands, faces of both the ejected and the voters.
5. **Sprint footage** — people running down hallways/stairs when the alarm hits. Shoot from a low or fixed angle; shaky chase-cam is fine, that's the aesthetic.
6. **Phone screens in context** — role reveal ("You are the INTRUDER" with their face behind it), taunt arriving, dead screen. Over-the-shoulder, 5s each.
7. **Dead-player couch** — ghosts reacting silently to a wrong accusation happening in front of them.
8. **Tasks being done earnestly** — fridge opened, thing found in bookshelf, whatever your list says. Comedy b-roll.
9. **Sonos speaker close-up while audio plays** — you need this insert shot for the anchor video and any Sonos-feature content.
10. **Lobby moment** — everyone on the couch joining with the room code, pre-game energy.
11. **Winner/loser end screens + group reaction** to both victory types.
12. **One intruder acting innocent** — chatting normally with someone they're about to kill. Dramatic irony b-roll, works in almost any edit.

### Nice-to-have

- Slow push-in shots of empty mansion rooms (tension inserts)
- Someone hiding badly (under table, behind curtain)
- Group photo/video at the end — usable as a "this could be your friend group" closer
- Screen recording of the host setting up tasks/locations (for idea #9 and tutorials)

### Practical notes

- **Audio:** the Sonos sounds are your best asset — make sure at least one fixed camera is near a speaker so you capture clean alarm audio with real reactions on top.
- **Consent:** 30-second group announcement that footage may be posted; give anyone an opt-out. Cheap insurance.
- **Volume math:** a 3–4 hour session with 4 fixed cams + floater + player clips = comfortably 20–30 postable clips. At 3–4 posts/week that's 6+ weeks of content.
- **Anti-slop rule:** if a clip needs a caption to explain why it's funny, cut it. Post only moments that read in the first 2 seconds.

## Posting notes (brief)

- Post the anchor video first, pin it, then Tier 1 clips 3–4x/week. Reuse the same alarm/bell audio across clips so it becomes recognizable.
- Every caption ends the same way: **susparty.com — free, no app, no accounts.** The "it's completely free" line is itself a hook; use it in at least one video's opening ("we built a free real-life Among Us and put it in a mansion").
- Reply to comments with the rules-in-30s video.
- Track which mechanic drives the most comments (alarm vs. kills vs. meetings) and double down — the footage list above gives you enough raw material to follow whatever hits.
